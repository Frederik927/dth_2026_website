# What are the disadvantages of open source software?

## Disadvantages 


Though open source software provides many possibilities it also carries potential hazards and challenges. One of the issues related to open source software is that it might be less user-friendly. This might affect the workflow of a company and make it challenging for employees to adopt the use of certain programs. Moreover, when using open source software, individuals and companies are often in a bad position in terms of legals rights due to the lack of liability and warranty, which is generally provided with proprietary software. Hence, by relying on open source software nobody is obliged to help you if you have any troubles, exposing the limitations of a community-driven projects. Furthermore, with open source software the implementation complexity might also be a burden. As you are responsible for configuration, integration and maintenance, you might end up compromising the productivity. Finally, the security risk cannot be overlooked. Hence, open source projects might be abandoned or go unmaintained causing an increased risk of security breaches. One of the most well-known open source projects is probably Wikipedia, which is a crowdsourced online encyclopaedia. Though it has several advantages, Wikipedia is also a primary example of the hazards of using open source software. Hence, anyone can create, edit or delete Wikipedia articles, weakening its reliability. Therefore, Wikipedia cannot be used by researchers or students to produce academic works as the authors cannot be accounted for. Wikipedia articles are also works-in-progress, meaning that information might not have been updated, reducing the deployment of Wikipedia information for official purposes. Finally, in regard to the security of Wikipedia, the open source format also makes it vulnerable for vandalism. Hence, people might delete or change information to harm others or promote their own agenda, posing a threat to individuals and the information society.
