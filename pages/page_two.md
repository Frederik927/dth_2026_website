# Disadvantages of open source software

## Disadvantages 


One of the issues of open source is that it might be less user-friendly. This might affect the workflow and make it challenging for employees to adopt the use of certain programs. Moreover, when using open source software, individuals and companies are often in a bad position in terms of legal rights due to the lack of liability and warranty. Hence, by relying on open source software nobody is obliged to help you if you have any troubles, exposing the limitations of a community-driven project. Furthermore, with open source software the implementation complexity might also be a burden. As you are responsible for configuration, integration and maintenance, you might end up compromising the productivity. Finally, open source projects might be abandoned or go unmaintained, causing vulnerabilities. One of the most well-known open source projects is probably Wikipedia, which is a crowdsourced online encyclopaedia. Though it has several advantages, Wikipedia is also a primary example of the hazards of using open source. Hence, anyone can create, edit or delete Wikipedia articles, weakening its reliability. Therefore, Wikipedia cannot be used by researchers or students to produce academic works as the authors cannot be accounted for. Wikipedia articles are also works-in-progress, meaning that information might not have been updated, reducing the deployment of Wikipedia for official purposes. Finally, the open source format also makes it vulnerable for vandalism. Hence, people might delete or change information to harm others or promote their own agenda, posing a threat to individuals and society.
